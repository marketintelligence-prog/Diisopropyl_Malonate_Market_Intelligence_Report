What it is

Diisopropyl malonate (DIPM) is the diisopropyl ester of malonic acid — a C–H-acidic 1,3-diester used as a three-carbon building block. Commercially it is a single-application intermediate: its active methylene, with carbon disulfide and 1,2-dichloroethane, builds the 1,3-dithiolane ring of the rice fungicide isoprothiolane (Nihon Nohyaku, FUJI-ONE) [16] nichino.co.jp [15] procurementresource.com. Minor uses are the hepatoprotectant malotilate [21] drugfuture.com and laboratory syntheses of 3-substituted coumarins and quinoxaline di-N-oxides [2] chemicalbook.com. Its commercial importance therefore rises and falls with rice-blast fungicide demand in Asia.

Industry Applications, End-Products & Application Market Sizes

1 · Agrochemicals — Isoprothiolane (≈ all DIPM volume)

Isoprothiolane is a systemic rice fungicide with plant-growth-regulator effects (rice blast, false smut; phospholipid-biosynthesis inhibitor). Originator Nihon Nohyaku sells it as FUJI-ONE: registered in Japan in 1974, in 27 countries by 2022, with major sales in China, Vietnam, India, Indonesia, Korea and Taiwan [16] nichino.co.jp [17] nichino-europe.com. It is not approved in the EU [18] aeru.herts.ac.uk.
